# Catalyst scorecard — 30 September 2026

**Neither thesis establishes an evidence-backed, dated forecast surprise within September 2027. Reframe the pitch; do not admit a combined short forecast.** This bounded pass identifies a clearer recovery hurdle, not evidence that it will fail. It does not prove that no catalyst exists. Three of six discovery queries returned no usable results, and several primary pages could not be refreshed; the access limitation narrows this conclusion.

Read with the [named expectations sheet](expectations_sheet_2026-10.md). **VERIFIED** = source statement checked; **MEASURED** = reproducible arithmetic on checked inputs; **FITTED** = calibrated to historical outcomes; **ASSUMED** = scenario or proposed decision rule; **UNVERIFIED** = unavailable, second-hand or unresolved. A verified broker assumption is not verified future performance. Proposed thresholds below are research rules fixed before results, not estimated likelihoods or confidence intervals.

## Ranked candidates and reveal conditions

### 1. A: less offset from wins / failure of the Q2 recovery

**Mechanism and contested assumption.** If the new account contributes fewer completed fee units, or later than expected, retained pricing cannot offset the missing transactions. Stephens 20 Aug explicitly forecasts US fee-unit growth of −1% in FY27 Q1 and +2% in Q2–Q4, with RPU +2%. Barclays 25 Aug expects Copart's win fully visible in FY27 Q2; its 11 Sep note still expects offsetting gains. These are **VERIFIED published assumptions**. They are not carrier-specific company guidance, and Stephens predates the September print.

**Observable/date/threshold.** Copart FY27 Q2 results, expected **February 2027**, exact date **UNVERIFIED**. Proposed **ASSUMED** failure threshold: compatible US fee-unit growth **≤0%**, rather than +2%, together with evidence of weaker or delayed won-account contribution. Use the same population; insurance sold-unit growth cannot substitute. **Kill:** fee-unit growth ≥+2% and realization of the expected account win by Q2. Intermediate results remain inconclusive. Lower units alone can also reflect industry claims or timing and would not identify A.

**Revision and status.** At exactly zero growth, unchanged forecast RPU and other branches imply **−$16.718m Q2 service** versus Stephens, but the resulting annual service estimate remains **$72.882m above JPM**. Calculation is **MEASURED conditional arithmetic**, shock **ASSUMED**. No evidence currently predicts the shortfall. **Rank: best observable watch candidate; not admitted.** Needs a current broker reaffirmation/update and an operational reason the win underdelivers.

### 2. A: retained-account seller concessions exceed the allowance

**Mechanism and contested assumption.** Lower seller charges on retained as well as incremental vehicles reduce fee revenue. Barclays already allows **25–50 bp** Copart take-rate pressure from the recent win and **10–20%** commission discounts. Its illustrative economics already suggest broad-base repricing. Repeating that calculation is not a new miss.

**Observable/date/threshold.** Contract-economics disclosure or sufficiently disaggregated Copart FY27 Q1/Q2 results, expected **November 2026 / February 2027**, exact dates **UNVERIFIED**. Proposed **ASSUMED** surprise threshold: **more than 50 bp** adverse impact under the *same take-rate definition and contract perimeter*, after buyer-fee offsets and price/mix changes. **Kill:** impact at or below the allowance, or offsetting fees preserve the published service forecast. Aggregate RPU alone does not identify seller concessions; RB Global's consolidated take rate is not Copart's rate.

**Revision and status.** Additional service loss would equal compatible affected GTV × incremental take-rate reduction, plus quantity/fee interactions. Affected GTV and exact public reconciliation are missing; **no defensible dollar revision**. Source expectation **VERIFIED**, future pricing shock **UNVERIFIED**. **Not admitted.**

### 3. A: another account moves, or known runoff lasts longer

**Mechanism and contested assumption.** A new award loss would reduce assignments beyond the already incorporated net contract drag. Barclays' dated allowance is **−2.5% to −3.5%** net insurance volume effect, distinct from total FY27 insurance growth of **−2.0% in its August note**. An extended existing transition could also delay recovery, but is not an indefinitely recurring annual loss.

**Observable/date/threshold.** Named carrier/auction award with effective date and affected exposure; otherwise the next RB Global automotive update (date **UNVERIFIED**) and Copart results. Surprise requires an incremental disclosed account loss outside the modeled transition, or an attributable net contract effect worse than **−3.5%** on the analyst's definition. **No named carrier renewal date within the horizon was verified.** State Farm's inherited modeled event is not a renewal notice. **Kill:** the known transition completes, other allocations remain stable and wins arrive as expected. Failure to find an announcement is not proof that no negotiations occur.

**Revision and status.** Without compatible exposure and terms, new-account dollar loss is **UNVERIFIED**. The existing **FITTED/ASSUMED** Progressive scenario is compared with genuinely flat sequential allocation below; it cannot be relabeled new information. **Not admitted.**

### 4. B: a dated procurement change accelerates aftermarket adoption

**Mechanism and contested assumption.** Expanded eligible parts or a procurement requirement could lower actual repair bills and divert marginal total losses to repair. Recycled displacement could separately lower donor contribution. None of the inspected brokers discloses a quantitative aftermarket adoption or recycled-displacement forecast; the model's 5.5-point basket shift is our own assumption.

**Observable/date/threshold.** Public carrier/DRP rollout naming incremental categories, effective date, exposure and source displaced, followed by comparable realized sourcing. **No such new rollout or date was verified.** Existing State Farm acceptance is not a new rollout. A numerical adoption *surprise* threshold is **not identifiable without a named expectation and common denominator**. Do not invent one. **Kill for joint units/price thesis:** adoption replaces OEM without material recycled contribution loss, or does not produce comparable-job savings. That may still support a narrower repair-cost mechanism.

**Revision and status.** National source-dollar share cannot be substituted for eligible-basket physical share. Adoption, displacement, threshold response and auction transmission remain **ASSUMED/UNVERIFIED**, with no defensible dated dollar revision. **Not admitted; structural context only.**

### 5. B: LKQ reveals recycled impairment while aftermarket/repairs hold

**Mechanism and contested assumption.** Lower sales contribution on substitutable recycled collision parts could induce lower donor bids before Copart recognizes weaker auction fees. The necessary signal is product divergence, not consolidated weakness. There is no inspected broker forecast for that divergence.

**Observable/date/threshold.** **29 October 2026, 08:00 ET**, the date previously verified in the [existing catalyst note](../docs/catalyst_assessment_2026-09-29/README.md); this pass's official-event refresh returned **HTTP 403**, so it is inherited verification, not newly reconfirmed. Proposed **ASSUMED** screening condition: recycled collision volume/contribution weakens while comparable aftermarket volume and repair activity are stable or rising, with management attribution to substitution and lower donor purchasing bids. Require compatible North American collision scope; Europe, FX, divestitures, ERP and consolidated margins are not substitutes. **Kill:** recycled contribution and donor bids remain resilient, or weakness is explained by the overall repair market rather than substitution.

**Revision and status.** This would justify reopening B, not itself force a numerical Copart miss. Donor exposure, bid response and influence on marginal auction prices remain unmeasured. **Not admitted; useful dated information window.**

### 6. B: estimating data and Copart fees reveal the joint effect

**Mechanism and contested assumption.** A matched adoption/disposition series could show repair savings diverting totals; weaker price-sensitive fees could show the donor-demand channel. Stephens' +2% all-US fee RPU is an explicit hurdle, but contains more than insurance auction-price exposure. JPM discloses no numerical forward RPU path.

**Observable/date/threshold.** CCC/Mitchell release date and required content **UNVERIFIED**; Copart Q1/Q2 windows above. A proposed initial fee screen is **US fee RPU ≤0% versus Stephens' +2%**, with contemporaneous comparable-job savings and recycled impairment. This is **ASSUMED**, not a source-based forecast. TLF need not fall outright, but a below-counterfactual claim requires an independently supported counterfactual. **Kill:** AM/recycled grow together, donor bids remain strong, or comparable fee growth meets the expectation. Lower blended RPU from seller/vehicle mix alone does not validate B.

**Revision and status.** No admitted decomposition isolates price-sensitive fees. A pure quarter-specific RPU miss can be calculated mechanically against Stephens; attribution and prediction remain absent. **Not admitted.**

## Decision and model gate

Lead future research with **A's recovery/offset test**; retain **B as structural optionality with two-sided price effects**. Neither currently passes from mechanism to predicted dated surprise. Three load-bearing objects remain: incremental contractual exposure/terms; incremental matched source displacement; causal selection/bid transmission. Public filings improve the first object's institutional plausibility, but none supplies an admitted central shock. The latter two remain sensitivities.

**Phase 4 is not triggered.** No new common-base combined forecast, effective-dated contract terms, quarterly adoption path or September-2027 model extension is justified by this pass. Existing model/workbook files were untouched; their tests and workbook verification were therefore not rerun. New offline source/denominator/arithmetic checks passed; these do not validate the economics.
