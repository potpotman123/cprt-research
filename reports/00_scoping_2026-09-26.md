## 1. Critique of the plan as written, before spending

1. **The deadline decides the order, and the deadline is unconfirmed.** If Oct 2 is the preliminary submission, six days remain. Only E10, the on-disk steps of E4/E1/E2/E7, and the human pulls (E6, E8) can land before it. E1 in full, E3, and E5 are finals-week work (Oct 12–22) at best. Please confirm the dates.
2. **Not every experiment can meet the explanation standard, and the doc's own test says so.** An explanation needs a named driver, a reason it moved, a reason it will move next, and a KPI consequence. Experiments with a *calendar* built into the driver satisfy that: E1 (model-year sales mix is history), E4 (sales three years ago are history), E2 (a policy cycle has a known length), E6 (dated fee steps). E3, E5 and E7 as written are regressions of one trending series on another with n ≈ 15–30; their likely output is another observation. I would run E3 as an accounting decomposition (labour share × wage growth + parts share × parts PPI) rather than a regression, and treat E5/E7 as cheap screens with an early kill.
3. **E2 step 1 is not runnable on Fast Track.** Twelve numeric points, half of them the COVID swing, no lag profile possible. Substitute Progressive's quarterly *frequency* change from its 10-Q MD&A (a numeric, quarterly, EDGAR-hosted series back to at least 2012), with VMT as the exposure control. Same mechanism, real data.
4. **E1 step 4 is unidentified as specified.** Fitting P_car and P_truck jointly to the *same* eight CCC statistics that pinned the single P adds two curves and no new targets. It only works if step 1–3 produce at least one body-level target (HLDI class-level severity/value, or a CCC/Mitchell total-loss-by-type statement). Add a kill rule: no body-level target → report bounds, do not fit.
5. **"Repair CPI" is not collision repair.** BLS `SETD` is motor-vehicle maintenance and repair, which includes routine service. For E3's numerator the better series is CCC's own repair cost per claim (TCOR), which is on the CCC pages already on disk. Use SETD for the long history, TCOR for the level check.
6. **E6's simulation input is missing.** The grid needs an ASP *distribution*, and Copart discloses only an average. The blueprint's lognormal-with-median-$5k is ASSUMED. The pitchable output of E6 is therefore the *shape* (convex ⇒ elasticity ≈ 0.5 by construction) plus dated steps, not a level forecast. Say so up front.
7. **Wayback is not the route for E6.** Verified above: the archived pages are stubs. Do not spend requests on it.
8. **E7's Census route has an auth trap.** USA Trade Online requires an account (prohibited). The Census API (`api.census.gov/data/timeseries/intltrade/exports/hs`) is keyless for light use; check its robots before touching it. The free part of E7 is the 10-K's international-buyer language, on disk.

### E4 — Off-lease supply → used-car CPI → the spread's value side

**Steps.**
1. *Local.* Build "supply of 2–4-year-old vehicles" for each quarter from the cohort sizes already in `light_vehicle_sales_by_year.csv` (sum of sales 2, 3 and 4 years earlier, monthly interpolated). This is the mechanism variable, not a distributed-lag search.
2. *Local.* Regress used-car CPI YoY (quarterly) on (a) that supply's YoY, (b) new-vehicle CPI YoY, (c) VMT YoY as a demand control. Fit on 1995–2019; test 2020–2026 out of sample; report the 2021–22 miss separately (that episode was a new-car supply shock, and the fit must not be allowed to learn it).
3. *Optional, 1–4 requests.* Lease penetration: Experian's automotive-finance press pages (check `experian.com/robots.txt`); if reachable, scale the supply variable by lease share.
4. *Local.* Forecast used-car CPI 2026Q4–2028Q4 from known sales, run through the spread regression, report the value-side ΔTLF by quarter.

**Cost.** 30–60k tokens; 0–4 requests.
**Alternatives.** Step 1–2 alone is the result; step 3 refines. A distributed-lag scan (the doc's version) is cheaper to code but will overfit; the constructed supply variable is one parameter with a story.
**Kill.** Supply coefficient wrong sign or insignificant on 1995–2019.
**Deliverable.** `reports/E4_offlease_values.md`; `data/csv/used_car_cpi_drivers.csv`; a forecast row for used-car CPI and the value-side ΔTLF by quarter.
**Recommendation.** Run second. Cheap, on-disk, and the driver has a three-year lead.

### E1 — The truck cohort wave

**Steps.**
1. *Local, first.* Classify `make_model` slugs into car / light truck (pickup, SUV, crossover, van) with a lookup table of ~200 model tokens on BEA/Ward's conventions (crossovers are light trucks). Compute the light-truck share of listed inventory by capture month, 2022-08 → 2026-09, from `raw/lotxml/` and `lot_snapshots`, with the unclassified share printed. Compare with the fleet roll's 57% (2024). **Gate:** if the two differ by more than 10 points, stop and reconcile the roll before anything else.
2. *Local.* Search the CCC pages already on disk for any total-loss or repair-cost statement by vehicle type (pickup, SUV, truck). Free, and if it exists it is the best body-level target available.
3. *Network, gated on robots.* `iihs.org/robots.txt`; then the vehicle-age research page (the redirect target on disk) and the insurance-losses-by-class tables. 5–20 requests.
4. *Network, gated on robots.* One public retained-value-by-segment table (iSeeCars / Edmunds article). 2–5 requests.
5. *Local.* If and only if steps 2–4 produce a body-level target: derive P_body(age) from severity/value by body under the CCC repair-cost distribution and refit with two P curves (`scripts/age_curves.py` as template, body dimension added). Otherwise report bounds.
6. *Local.* Roll to 2030; report ΔTLF and ΔASP by year attributable to body mix (the ASP half needs E8's ratio).

**Cost.** Step 1: 30–50k tokens, 0 requests. Full: 150–250k tokens, 10–30 requests.
**Alternatives.** Step 1 alone confirms or refutes the roll's body split and is worth doing before Oct 2. Steps 3–6 are finals-week work.
**Kill.** Step 1 gate fails; or no body-level target found (then no fit); or P_truck ≈ P_car within tolerance (then price story only).
**Deliverable.** `reports/E1_truck_cohort.md`; `data/csv/listed_body_share_monthly.csv` (step 1); `hldi_class_losses.csv`, `value_by_age_body.csv`, `age_curves_by_body.csv` (if reached).
**Recommendation.** Step 1 now; the rest after E10/E4/E2.

### E2 — Insurance affordability → filing behaviour → the claims term

**Steps.**
1. *Network.* Progressive 10-Q/10-K MD&A, 2016–2026 (~40 filings, EDGAR, descriptive UA): extract the quarterly personal-auto frequency and severity change sentences. Regex on a fixed phrase ("frequency … decreased/increased X%"), hand-checked. Alternatively, GEICO sentences (on disk) and Allstate's quarterly supplement if its host's robots allows.
2. *Local.* Regress frequency change on VMT change (exposure) and insurance CPI YoY lagged 0–8 quarters. The affordability effect is the part of frequency decline not explained by miles.
3. *Network, 2–6 requests.* Deductible-share and coverage-penetration points: CCC pages (on disk plus re-fetch of 2026 pages), Triple-I public pages, IRC uninsured-motorist releases. Expect 4–6 annual points; this is a level check, not a regression input.
4. *Local.* Filing-propensity model: claims = accidents (VMT × trend) × filing rate(insurance CPI, lag from step 2). Forecast the claims term by fiscal quarter from the insurance CPI path already on disk (it has been falling since April 2024's peak).
5. *Local.* Cross-check against PGR policies-in-force growth (on disk).

**Cost.** 100–160k tokens; 40–50 requests (small filings, but many).
**Alternatives.** Step 1 with PGR only and 2019–2026 (28 filings) halves the requests and still spans one full cycle. Fast Track stays as a corroborating headline series only.
**Kill.** No lag structure after controlling for VMT; or wrong-sign filing elasticity.
**Deliverable.** `reports/E2_affordability_claims.md`; `data/csv/pgr_frequency_quarterly.csv`, `claims_term_drivers.csv`; a claims-term forecast row by fiscal quarter with the lag stated.
**Recommendation.** Run third. The claims term is the largest hand input in FY27 units.
