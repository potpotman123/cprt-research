## 1. The unresolved question, in my words

Copart's share price embeds some analyst's path for FY27 legacy service revenue. That path is a handful of numbers: how far US insurance units fall while the lost account runs off and when they recover, how much insurance ASP and fees add per unit, how fast international grows, and what the ACV deal adds. The two theses say those numbers are too high because (A) insurers can now move volume to, or extract seller concessions from, a credible second auction, and (B) cheaper aftermarket repairs are quietly shrinking both the flow of total losses and dismantlers' bids for them. The question is not whether either mechanism exists. It is: **which specific number in a named FY27 forecast is wrong, by how much, and what dated public observation before September 2027 will show it?** If no such number can be named and dated, the project is structural research and the pitch must say so.

Three things make this hard, and the repository has already established them. First, the benchmark we hold (JPM, 11 Sep 2026: FY27 service revenue $4,061m, ex-ACV, +2.3%) is a total; the driver assumptions behind it have not been recorded, so nothing can yet be called a miss. Second, the model's own reference sits above that total ($4,093m under the CCC calibration), so a thesis effect "versus our reference" overstates any gap to the Street. Third, both theses in their current model form depend on assumed response coefficients (allocation paths and event probabilities for A; displacement, donor exposure and bid transmission for B) that no evidence file supports as central values.

## 2. Competing explanations for FY26's unit decline and price resilience

FQ4 FY26 facts (call, 10 Sep 2026; SEC-filed exhibit): US insurance sold units −7.5%; US insurance assignments +2.3% excluding one customer; US assignments −5%; US inventory −3.4% with faster cycle times and less aged inventory; collision claim frequency −3.4% and moderating; CY2026 Q2 total-loss frequency 23.3% vs 22.4%; US insurance ASP +3.7% vs Manheim +2.8%; global FY26 units −5.5%, or −3.1% ex-CAT (Helene/Milton in FY25).

1. **Account movement plus resilient salvage economics** (the strongest opposing reading, and management's). One large carrier moved most of its volume away between April and July 2026; everything else grew; record total-loss frequency and rising severity keep supply growing; prices beat used-car indices because of buyer liquidity. Under this reading the unit decline laps in FY27Q4 and nothing else is wrong. The identification of that carrier as Progressive rests on the inherited expert-call quote bank and Barclays' "Insurer B", not on any Copart disclosure.
2. **Contestable allocation (thesis A).** The loss is the first visible instance of insurers treating the two auctions as substitutes. RB Global's Q2 2026 release (on disk today) attributes 11% automotive unit growth to net market-share gains and cites automotive pricing incentives tied to higher transaction volumes inside its 110 bp take-rate decline. Barclays' contract arithmetic (25 Aug 2026) is consistent with a 20% seller-commission discount applied across a whole carrier base. The unproven step is whether more of this is coming than the Street already prices.
3. **Aftermarket substitution (thesis B).** Repair-cost relief at the margin removes total losses that would otherwise reach auction and lowers dismantlers' donor bids. Observed total-loss frequency and ASP are both rising, so B can only be true relative to a counterfactual, which is not publicly observable. The 2022–25 adoption gain (CCC aftermarket dollar share 18.0% → 22.7%) is already in the revenue base; only incremental 2026–27 adoption counts.
4. **Industry frequency.** Collision claim frequency fell through 2025 and is still falling; this shrinks the pool for both auctions and is not Copart-specific.
5. **Timing and inventory.** Faster cycle times and aged-inventory clean-up make sold units and assignments diverge quarter to quarter; a sold-unit decline can overstate an assignment decline.
6. **Mix and CAT.** FY25's hurricane units were low-value and inflated the comparison base; their absence raises ASP and lowers units at once, which mimics "price resilience with volume weakness" without any thesis.

Explanations 1, 4, 5 and 6 together account for the FY26 print without either thesis. The theses therefore have to be shown in FY27 numbers, against a forecast that already contains explanation 1.
