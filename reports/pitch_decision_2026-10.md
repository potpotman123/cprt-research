# Copart: expectations recovered; catalyst short not established

**30 September 2026 — PM decision: reframe the pitch.** Neither competitive convergence nor aftermarket substitution currently identifies an evidence-supported forecast error with a public reveal inside September 2027. That is the result of a bounded, partly access-limited investigation, not proof that no catalyst exists.

**What is being contested.** JPM's 11 September forecast is **$4,061m FY27 legacy service revenue, explicitly excluding ACV**, approximately **+2.3%**. Its underlying quarterly insurance units, ASP and fees are not disclosed. Stephens' 20 August note supplies a more testable operating path: **US fee units −1% in Q1, then +2% in Q2–Q4; US fee RPU +2% throughout**, producing **$4,150.6m** services. These are dated, differently scoped expectations, not interchangeable consensus measures. [Expectations and source locators](expectations_sheet_2026-10.md).

**Competition is already in the forecast.** Barclays' September note allows **2.5–3.5% net insurance-volume drag** from contract changes and **25–50 bp take-rate pressure**. The new Copart 10-K verifies non-exclusive, limited-notice seller arrangements and a fall in insurer seller mix from **81% to 79%**. It does not name another lost account or identify when weaker terms take effect. RB Global's land totals combine businesses; its acquisition and CEO change date to **March and August 2023**, respectively. The evidence supports contestability, not identical offerings or an identifiable equilibrium share.

**Aftermarket adoption is not yet a forecastable joint unit/price loss.** The reports inspected disclose no adoption or recycled-displacement assumptions to contest. Historic source-share gains do not identify new FY27 exposure. Comparable-job savings, recycled donor contribution and marginal auction-bid transmission remain unmeasured. More repaired cars, OEM displacement, rebuilder demand and donor scarcity can offset the proposed price decline. The existing large case remains a stress: **−$90.66m versus its own CCC reference, only −$58.59m versus JPM**, with no supported quarterly rollout.

**The strongest test is failure of the Q2 recovery.** Copart's FY27 Q2 report is expected in **February 2027**, exact date unconfirmed. Pre-specify **US fee-unit growth ≤0% versus Stephens' +2%** and require evidence of delayed/weaker won-account contribution to attribute it to A. At exactly zero, retaining its RPU and other lines reduces Q2 service by **$16.72m**. Annual services would still be **$72.88m above JPM**. This is conditional arithmetic, not a predicted miss. **Kill:** comparable fee units recover by at least 2%, wins arrive, and pricing stays resilient. LKQ's **29 October** call remains an inherited confirmed observation window for B, but a product-level divergence is neither scheduled nor forecast by the evidence.

**Recommendation.** Present this as a structural research piece unless fresh evidence establishes the recovery shortfall. If competition format requires a 3–12-month trade, prioritize a different catalyst-backed idea. A long-leaning recovery/RPU-floor framing is worth testing against current expectations and valuation, but this work does not validate a long either. Do not extend the deadline to await a full post-transition comparison: FY27 Q4 overlaps the loss anniversary; the first fully post-transition FY28 Q1 print may lie beyond the horizon.

No forecast survives the integration gate, so no combined engine case or workbook was changed. The DCF should receive **no new evidence-backed revenue shock** from this pass.
