## 4. A measurable test, without inventing a forecast

**ASSUMED diagnostic:** if FY27 Q2 US fee-unit growth is **0% instead of Stephens' +2%**, with its RPU and every other forecast line retained, US service falls by:

`$852.6m × (1 / 1.02 − 1) = −$16.718m`.

Global Q2 service becomes **$987.582m** instead of $1,004.3m; annual service becomes **$4,133.882m**, still **$72.882m above JPM**. Arithmetic is **MEASURED**; the shock is **ASSUMED**, not a prediction. JPM quarterly services are unavailable, so no JPM Q2 service miss can be computed. This is a named driver sensitivity, not a new engine case, thesis attribution or consensus miss.

The three binding evidence gaps are: **(1)** incremental carrier allocation/terms and their effective dates versus the already expected transition; **(2)** a forward, compatible aftermarket path separating OEM from recycled displacement; **(3)** economic transmission from that displacement to marginal total-loss decisions and donor bids. The first can sometimes be resolved by contract or operating disclosures. The latter two require matched source/disposition and donor economics; the current public aggregates do not identify them. Insurance revenue allocation changes magnitude, but cannot establish surprise or timing by itself.
