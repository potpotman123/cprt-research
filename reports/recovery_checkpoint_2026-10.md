**Question:** Can either proposed mechanism establish an incremental error in a named FY27 legacy-service forecast, reveal that error publicly within September 2027, and quantify the estimate change without relying on an assumed response to make the answer appear?

The target is forecast error in compatible service dollars and operating drivers, not historical explanatory fit, total-company revenue with an unresolved acquisition perimeter, or a percentage change against our own reference. The decision is whether this research supports a catalyst-led competition short, should instead supply structural context, or merits a different investment framing.

The required recovery documents were read in the requested order. Their joint conclusion remains the starting point: coherent conditional architecture, insufficient evidence for a near-term short. The archived CCC reference is $4,093.08m service revenue; the larger aftermarket case is $4,002.41m, a $90.66m own-reference effect and $58.59m gap to JPM's dated $4,061m forecast. These are assumed/fitted scenario outputs, not measured misses.

Several explanations fit FY26's declining units and resilient prices. **Account movement plus resilient salvage economics** is the strongest opposing reading: the September call reports insurance sold units −7.5%, customer-adjusted insurance assignments +2.3%, insurance ASP +3.7%, and rising total-loss frequency. A contestable duopoly could also produce those results, but requires further losses or weaker terms than already forecast to become incremental downside. Aftermarket substitution could reduce total losses relative to a counterfactual; simultaneous increases in aftermarket and recycled use, stronger rebuilder economics and donor scarcity can weaken or reverse its auction-price effect. Industry claim frequency, catastrophe comparisons, inventory conversion and vehicle mix also separate assignments, sales and prices. None can be discarded by fitting the reported quarter.

The local-first pipeline is to recover named assumptions and their exact populations, check filing facts and existing share observations, perform the approved bounded public discovery, then ask whether any observation discriminates between those explanations. Direct carrier disclosures and broker schedules are more precise and cheaper than new listing collection. Matched repair and donor records would identify the aftermarket channel better than aggregate regressions, but access is not established. Further fitting against aggregate levels would not solve that problem.
