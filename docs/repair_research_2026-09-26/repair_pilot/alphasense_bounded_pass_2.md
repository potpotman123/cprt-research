## Critical accounting distinction

Catalog price, shop acquisition cost and insurer-allowed repair charge are different quantities. Manufacturer/dealer support may lower the shop's acquisition cost without reducing the insurer's allowed repair bill. If the benefit is retained as shop margin, it does not directly alter the repair-versus-total decision through the repair-cost channel. A sourcing-price model must distinguish these amounts rather than assume full pass-through.

## Competing explanations and cheap discriminating tests

- Genuine insurer-cost saving: matched final insurer-approved estimate and supplier invoice show a lower allowed charge for the relevant operation. Compare same part/work and record allowance, purchase cost and credits separately.
- Shop/dealer margin support: acquisition cost falls while allowed charge stays unchanged. The same paired records distinguish this from insurer savings.
- Sourcing/approval effect: OEM substitution occurs at a similar allowed amount, changing installed-part mix without comparable repair-cost savings. Compare initial estimate, supplements and final approved lines.
- Sample composition: observed savings reflect different carriers, geographies, parts or damage scope. Match those characteristics before attributing differences to age/body type.

Public retail catalogs cannot resolve these alternatives. A few public estimates can audit work scope but generally cannot establish realized acquisition cost. A useful matched record would contain vehicle/year/body/trim, damage scope, estimate date/stage, insurer-approved line amount, actual sourcing, supplier invoice cost and credits, plus labor/paint. Final invoice/approved estimate must not be presumed identical to what the insurer ultimately paid.

## Model decision

Keep sourcing and transaction-price effects as uncalibrated sensitivity assumptions. Do not use 60/40 as an observed population share or the previous 1% repair premium as validated. No identified age/body-specific effect, magnitude, 3–12 month catalyst or earnings impact emerges from this small pass. The sourcing mechanism is now better supported, but it is not yet a standalone CPRT thesis.

Before any large invoice collection, first demonstrate that even a few public records contain the fields needed to distinguish insurer savings from shop margin. If they do not, require access to paired records or leave the mechanism as a sensitivity. Large OCR/document review would require user approval with scope, expected resource cost, reason and cheaper alternatives.
