## Admission standard for a future record

Required to reconcile observed amounts: common repair identifier (anonymized in analysis), vehicle/year/trim, estimate stage and date, final insurer-approved part line amount, supplier part number/source/quantity, purchase invoice, returns and credits, and a record tying the installed part to the final repair. Separate tax, freight, deductible and betterment from the part comparison. Unobserved rebates cannot be assumed zero; rebates paid to the dealer cannot automatically be assigned to the shop.

Even a complete invoice paired with an allowance measures an observed spread, not necessarily discount pass-through. For pass-through, require a documented before/after quote or revised allowance for the same work, with any other changes identified. A generic MSRP-minus-cost comparison is not a causal measure of savings passed to an insurer.

## Decision

Do not fund a broad public-invoice scrape at this point. The gate has not yielded the necessary fields. Public estimates remain useful for identifying omitted operations and separating initial from supplemented repair scope. They do not support a sourcing-pass-through coefficient.

Keep two empirical questions separate:
1. What amount does the insurer allow for comparable repair work? This is closest to the repair-versus-total mechanism and may be studied with estimates, with selection and timing caveats.
2. Why is that amount different, and who retains sourcing discounts? This requires procurement records and a counterfactual, and is optional unless the thesis depends on the sourcing mechanism.

For CPRT, do not make procurement-margin attribution a prerequisite for every repair-cost comparison. Return priority to comparable work scope and insurer-allowed amounts; carry sourcing as an uncertainty until better evidence exists. A larger document campaign, OCR or outreach remains unstarted and would require a justified proposal and user authorization.
