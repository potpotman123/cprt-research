## Itemized estimate feasibility

No representative itemized claims dataset has been secured. Previously obtained public examples can demonstrate line-item structure, not estimate a segment premium.

1. Small public-document discovery: estimating-system training examples, collision-repair case studies, and public regulatory exhibits. Label simulated training documents separately from real initial estimates, supplements and final invoices.
2. Admit a document only when vehicle/model year, estimate date, damage scope, operations, parts sourcing, labor and totals are interpretable. Remove personal identifiers from analytical outputs. Use embedded PDF text first; manually inspect a handful of pages if needed. No large OCR campaign.
3. Match common work (same components and operations) to compare conditional costs. Separately report additional operations actually present. Geography, labor rates, age, equipment and estimate stage remain potential confounders.
4. Require observed repair frequencies from a broader source to turn conditional costs into average costs. Public estimates are selected and may overrepresent disputes, unusual repairs or repairable vehicles. They cannot independently identify total-loss frequency.
5. If sufficiently comparable public examples cannot be found, stop this route rather than accumulate unrelated anecdotes. A de-identified shop/estimator extract or controlled same-scope estimates would then be needed. Neither access nor outreach is assumed or initiated.

Current research priority: corroborate transaction-price matching and look for age/body-specific sourcing evidence in existing AlphaSense documents before expanding retail-price collection. Keep uncertain inputs as scenarios. No forecast changes.
