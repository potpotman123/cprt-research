# Older-model replacement-parts check

September 26, 2026

## Result and scope

Completed a bounded follow-up search for comparable repair operations. No sufficiently matched complete repair-estimate pair was obtained. As a narrower fallback, compared OEM replacement parts for a 2015 Toyota Camry LE and a 2015 Toyota RAV4 XLE using the same dealer catalog, Reliable Toyota Missouri. This produces a reproducible component-price comparison, not a completed replacement-bundle quote or a calibrated total-loss model input.

Both models are eleven model years behind 2026, but catalog prices do not observe repairs performed on actual eleven-year-old vehicles. Calendar prices, trim, manufacturing origin and parts status are recorded. The selected RAV4 parts are Canada-built variants, consistently across the fender, hood and insulation. The Camry and RAV4 trims are not identical, and no VIN-level fitment confirmation was performed. Listed sale prices are public asking prices before shipping/tax, not insurer/shop negotiated invoice prices. Search-index freshness differs by page; this is not a simultaneous executable dealer quote.

## Defined baskets and calculations

Basket A: replace one left fender and one hood panel; reuse existing insulation. Camry $724.10; RAV4 $718.53. Ratio 0.9923: RAV4 costs 0.8% less for these two parts.

Basket B: same replacements, but also replace one hood insulation pad on each vehicle. Camry $921.40; RAV4 $1,113.88. Ratio 1.2089: RAV4 costs 20.9% more for these three parts.

Neither basket includes hinges, fasteners, seals, preparation, paint, adjacent-panel blending, labor, diagnostic/calibration work, taxes or shipping. Neither assumes those costs are zero. The baskets hold listed components constant; they do not assert that those components constitute a complete repair or that insulation requires replacement whenever the hood does.

The cancellation in Basket A does not validate the earlier 1.01 multiplier: one selected pair and two panels can be near parity by coincidence. Basket B also does not validate 1.21 as an average repair multiplier. Its relevance is identifying insulation replacement/reuse as a consequential scope item for this particular basket. A higher-priced component's incidence must be measured before it affects the average.

## Why the model threshold cannot be applied to the parts ratio

The existing body-mix sensitivity changes revenue sign at approximately 1.18 for the assumed full light-truck/car repair-cost multiplier, holding other assumptions fixed. A parts-only premium is a different variable.

Illustrative arithmetic only: adding the same assumed $600 of non-parts costs to Basket B produces $1,521.40 for the Camry and $1,713.88 for the RAV4, a 12.7% full-basket premium rather than 20.9%. The $600 is not an estimate of actual labor/paint costs. It demonstrates dilution when an expensive component is only part of the total. Different labor requirements could instead increase or reduce the premium.

More generally, full-cost ratio = (RAV4 selected parts + RAV4 other costs) / (Camry selected parts + Camry other costs). We have observed neither other-cost term. Furthermore, the prior model covers all light trucks, including pickups and minivans, while this comparison covers one crossover. No catalog ratio has been substituted into the model or used to generate a new revenue forecast.

## Search disposition and cost control

This follow-up used ten targeted search queries and bounded page reads, no OCR, paid access, outreach or bulk collection. The initial older-model estimate search located a 2015 RAV4 municipal packet with rear-bumper replacement, a differently aged Camry example, and a CCC training example for a 2008 Accord. These do not establish comparable vehicle age, damage and final repair stage. Municipal packet access failed, and a training example is not a new observed repair transaction.

The fallback initially targeted fender, bumper and lamp components. Several bumper/lamp pages were inaccessible. Hood pages were accessible, so the resulting two-panel basket is an exploratory availability-based selection, not a preregistered representative collision bundle. Hood insulation was a conditional sensitivity discovered on those pages. Both high and low SUV price differences are retained. No claim of random sampling or independent replication is made.

## What this changes

Evidence now supports component-specific heterogeneity: in this one same-year/same-vendor OEM comparison, the crossover is more expensive for one panel and cheaper for another. It does not identify average severity or its distribution around the total-loss boundary. A single general body-type repair multiplier remains insufficiently supported.

The cheapest useful continuation is to obtain complete, consistently scoped estimating operations for this exact model pair and a second independent pair, including ancillary parts and insulation reuse. Repeating broad searches for unrelated total invoices has low value. After that, damage-pattern frequencies and OEM/aftermarket/recycled part shares would be needed to turn component bundles into a fleet assumption. A larger collection is not initiated here.
