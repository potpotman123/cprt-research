# Secular short candidate ranking — 2026-09-28

**29 September execution:** [Quantified aftermarket bridge](../../model/aftermarket_bridge_2026-09-29/README.md) implements the selected thesis and its offsets in the successor model. It produces conditional unit/RPU downside, tests sign reversals and reverse-solves a 3% dated-JPM service miss. It does not establish a causal forecast or a guaranteed downward feedback loop.

Judgment, not adopted forecast. Strongest differentiated candidate from existing architecture: persistent competition from manufactured aftermarket parts weakens recycled donor economics, potentially lowering both auction prices and insurer total-loss propensity. Strongest established secular mechanism among candidates: diffusion of crash avoidance, but total-loss-specific effect and next-year magnitude unproven. No candidate yet satisfies durability + material consensus delta + dated catalyst.

Mechanism: if same-component aftermarket substitution reduces recycled sell-through or margins, dismantlers' net expected proceeds per donor may fall and bids may weaken. If realized salvage prices fall, price-linked Copart fees fall; if insurer expected net salvage also falls, repair threshold V-S rises, potentially reducing total-loss assignments. Neither observed recycled pieces per appraisal nor aftermarket dollar shares establish these links. Source competition may also benefit rebuilders via lower input costs, supporting their auction bids. Export competition, supply scarcity, pricing power, and more repairs can offset dismantler weakness. Lower units/RPU are conditional, not an automatic hedge.

CCC 2025 aftermarket count 3.2→3.3, recycled 0.6→0.5 and dollar shares 21→22.7%,10.7→10.5% are historical aggregate clues (MIX_SEGMENTATION.md), not same-component substitution. Broad age mix alone fails to explain replacement-count decline; damage selection remains unresolved (COHORT_TEST.md).

Other candidates:
- Coverage participation: stronger potential unit mechanism than small-claim nonreporting. Dropping own-damage coverage could divert total-loss vehicles from insurer assignments, but liability recovery/noninsurance consignment can recapture vehicles. Affordability can reverse; persistence unproven.
- ADAS: durable diffusion and evidence of fewer claims, but known thesis, uneven crash-type/severity effects and slow fleet turnover. Do not apply all-claim reductions directly to total losses; repair complexity provides counterweight.
- SUV/pickup mix/fleet aging: existing short-horizon roll weak; high residual values, repair costs and salvage economics compete. Don't promote preliminary premium assumptions to established secular short.
- Carrier allocation: potentially persistent level loss is not endless share erosion. Calibrated quote model not validated assignments; continuing customer exposure shifts require vehicle exposures and total-loss propensity, not premium shares alone.
- Fee/service lapping: possible earnings catalyst/slowdown, not independent secular deterioration. Requires dated fee and adoption evidence; prior price increase alone never implies negative future RPU.

Catalyst conditions over next four quarters: like-for-like salvage bids soften; price-driven fee contribution slows; later insurer expected salvage revisions affect assignments; evidence of sustained lower exposure-adjusted total-loss flow despite normalizing claims. These are measurable conditional milestones, not announced future events. If bids stay strong or assignments recover, weaken/reject the short. First task is exploit already-held sell-side/transcript evidence for matched price/recycler-vs-rebuilder differentiation, not refit damage elasticity or bulk scrape.
