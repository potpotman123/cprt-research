## Decision

Build one reconciled US unit model, with insurance and non-insurance branches. Within each branch, distinguish consignment and purchased vehicles before converting units to service revenue. Total US unit statistics cannot automatically be multiplied by service-only RPU: purchased vehicles can contribute to the company unit denominator while their sales revenue has a different accounting basis.

The first milestone is a correctly defined historical growth bridge. Absolute units, channel revenue shares and a validated assignment-to-sale lag remain unresolved. Do not present a complete absolute unit/revenue forecast until those starting levels are identified or explicitly normalized.

## 2. Issues found that change how we use prior findings

### Claims frequency is not claim count

`Claim count = earned insured exposure × claim frequency` for compatible definitions. Earlier FQ4 arithmetic combines a -3.4% frequency change with TLF growth from 22.4% to 23.3%, producing a +0.481% pool proxy. That is a count-growth estimate only under flat relevant exposure, compatible populations and timing. The current evidence does not establish all those conditions. The +0.481% is therefore conditional, not measured industry pool growth.

### Exact share growth differs from the additive gap

The old panel calls `Copart growth - industry pool growth` the share term. It is an additive growth gap. The exact relative residual is `(1+Copart growth)/(1+pool growth)-1`, under matched definitions. The audit preserves both and checks the product reconstructs company growth. Even the exact residual includes timing and measurement error; it is not automatically competitive share.

### Existing CY2025 claims about share conflict

`decomposition.csv` uses -2.35% Copart growth, an arithmetic average of four fiscal-quarter growth rates. The panel script uses a month-overlap-weighted -3.4833% for calendar 2025. Against the same -2.9% CCC valuation count, these yield different signs. Neither averaging procedure gives exact annual volume growth without prior-period volume weights. CCC valuations and Copart ex-CAT sales also need population reconciliation. Do not repeat the earlier +0.55pp residual as measured proof of no share loss.

### Catastrophe treatment is not consistently matched

The historical panel often pairs Copart ex-CAT units with CCC all-coverage claims/TLF. Removing catastrophes from one side only can move the apparent share residual. Maintain separate as-reported and ex-CAT checks; require compatible industry treatment before interpreting the latter.

### A useful but conditional channel-weight cross-check

If Q4 US total units -5.7%, insurance -7.5% and non-insurance +0.2% cover exactly the same mutually exclusive population, the prior-year insurance unit weight is `(-5.7-0.2)/(-7.5-0.2)=76.62%`. This derives from the weighted growth identity. It is an approximate FY25Q4 unit weight from rounded inputs, NOT current insurance share, global share or service revenue share. Verify source scope before adoption; do not overwrite the prior 80/20 assumption yet.

## 3. Complete initial operating architecture

### Insurance branch

1. Set a compatible total claim count or a normalized claims base and forecast exposure × claim frequency. If observed total claim counts are used, do not multiply exposure again.
2. Allocate the claim pool across carriers with weights summing to one. Use provisional weights only with their premium/exposure/claim meaning identified. Progressive's share trajectory remains a scenario until its exposure basis is audited.
3. Allocate each carrier's claims across age/body/loss types. Existing fleet/claim weights can supply a common initial profile. Claim weights must be normalized; do not infer carrier differences without evidence. Collision, comprehensive and CAT branches require non-overlapping definitions, including recovered theft vehicles where relevant.
4. Damage selection produces TLF and conditional salvage values from the same states. Aggregate eligible total losses by carrier; routing treatment must match the allocation denominator.
5. Apply the friend's carrier allocation paths to those eligible losses. Aggregate share is derived, not applied again. Contract loss/win counts must not also be added separately if the allocation paths already include them.
6. Convert assignments to completed sales using a documented lag/conversion schedule, with pre-forecast assignments/opening inventory and non-sale exits. If lag cannot be identified, show a small set of explicit timing cases, not a claimed measured lag.

### Non-insurance branch

Begin with one non-insurance unit stream anchored to compatible historical growth/weights, with an explicit operating-volume assumption. Split dealer versus other sellers only when usable evidence materially improves the forecast. Do not apply insurer TLF to this stream. A volume assumption here is permissible as a transparent first version; no unexplained total service-revenue growth plug is allowed.

### US total and revenue interface

US total sold units = insurance sold units + non-insurance sold units. Within each branch, identify consignment versus purchased vehicle scope. Consignment services use relevant transaction/activity fees; purchased-vehicle sales revenue stays outside the requested service-only forecast. Preserve any service activity that belongs to purchased transactions only if accounting/source evidence supports it. Complete unit coverage is not yet complete revenue coverage.

## 5. What blocks which milestone

**Can do with current evidence:** specify the complete chain, produce a normalized historical insurance-unit bridge, audit carrier source definitions and identify reconciliation errors. Initial claims/allocations can be explicit scenarios rather than claimed observations.

**Needed before a defensible unit forecast:** consistent recent sold-unit/assignment definitions; claims frequency versus count/exposure distinction; period and catastrophe reconciliation; better-supported carrier weighting and event timing. These are source-matching tasks first, not reasons to start a large scrape.

**Needed before absolute units and dollars:** a credible absolute unit or realized-fee anchor, a compatible insurance/non-insurance split and purchase/consignment mapping. A base-period normalization can be used if clearly labeled and constrained by independent evidence. One revenue number cannot identify both units and fees.

**Not necessary now:** detailed underwriting models for every carrier, another large parts-price scrape, a fully disaggregated international thesis or an enormous listing sample.
