# Thesis and model architecture audit — 1 October 2026

**Conclusion: the revenue model now supports a common quarterly test of the memo's recovery arguments. It does not yet establish an evidence-backed bear forecast.** The main implementation gaps were a carrier reference that replayed earlier allocations, static carrier terms, and an aftermarket calculation that could not accept the premium/carrier/fleet scenario or a quarterly adoption path. Those gaps are repaired. The remaining material weaknesses are empirical: coverage behavior, carrier exposure/allocation, aftermarket displacement and selection/bid responses.

Scope: FY27 **legacy service revenue**, with research depth in US insurance. This audit uses the user's stated bull claims, not a reconstruction of sell-side consensus. It inspected existing evidence and source failures, traced each claim through inputs and equations, tested opposing mechanisms, and ran all 16 combinations of four drivers against a shared historical ledger. It did not refit observed history or adjust coefficients to achieve a short target. Earnings, cash flow, valuation and a verified catalyst remain outside this revenue implementation.

Nominal premium levels alone do not measure affordability. | Explicit premium/income, premium/vehicle-value and deductible/income burdens drive bounded coverage and repairable-filing probabilities with lag. ## Architecture changes completed

1. **Carrier reference and dated terms.** Kept historical comparison modes reproducible, made explicit quarterly carrier weights available, validated allocation names/paths and added quarter-specific carrier fee/value/repair terms. New diagnostic outputs expose allocation, selected TLF, reported versus pre-incremental-filing claims, ASP and fees. The proposed simplified runoff now runs jointly with the other theses.
2. **Premium consistency.** Both public engine entry points now apply the premium adapter; previously a direct call to the operating calculation could omit the attached premium block. Existing double-counting guards remain. The reference's current high premium burden is not treated as a fresh shock. The adapter is a uniform eligible-pool approximation, not a cohort-level policy model.
3. **Fleet isolation.** Added explicit calendar-weight overrides to compare the existing roll with frozen same-quarter cohort mass. The CCC anchor and source data remain unchanged; there was no new calibration.
4. **Joint aftermarket path.** Added a four-quarter sourcing equilibrium conditional on the same premium/carrier/fleet configuration. It preserves repair-demand, recycler/rebuilder and scarcity offsets, and separates disposition-responsive claims from a locked population. The old standalone bridge remains reproducible. Macro coverage-driven auction-price feedback and detailed third-party/noninsurance rerouting remain outside this closure.
5. **Contribution accounting.** All 16 scenarios share the historical service ledger. Every contribution reconciles to a common revenue endpoint; interaction is allocated over every possible ordering. No independent scenario losses are simply added together.

## Remaining model risks, ranked

**High: baseline and causal calibration.** Carrier weights originate in auto-share proxies rather than matched claim exposures; allocation paths are inferred. The CCC fit identifies frequencies and composition, not the response to future repair/price changes. Premium coverage/filing responses and donor-bid transmission are assumed. These are the main sensitivities supporting the dollars, not evidence gaps that more bookkeeping can close.

**High: scope and offsets.** The 90% US insurance share of service dollars is assumed. The 79% global insurance share of vehicles processed is a different denominator. International continuation contributes **$89.91m** against flat international activity/RPU, a substantial offset retained in every audit scenario. Other US, purchased and acquired streams have simpler or unresolved treatment. No total-company EPS impact follows from this service bridge alone.

**High: timing and catalyst.** Adoption fractions are cumulative quarterly assumptions; the PGR path is sale-equivalent. No contract calendar, coverage cohort observation or adoption event establishes when a surprise occurs. A physical assignment-to-sale lag needs opening inventory and vintage economics; applying another lag to the current share path would double count timing. The new ramp permits a dated hypothesis but does not validate its dates.

**Medium: fleet and price composition.** EPA model-year subtype shares are transferred to calendar-year birth totals; 2026–27 births retain prior flat assumptions. Survival and claim propensity combine mileage, coverage and filing effects; no direct policy decomposition is identified. Broad body mappings and 7+ age splits remain proxies. Current posted fees applied historically, buyer/seller mix and ancillary revenue are not all observed. Constant-composition diagnostics remain distinct from aggregate ASP/RPU.

The highest-value next observations are matched coverage exposure around renewals/payoff; carrier allocation with dates, geography and fees; and matched component substitution/donor economics. CCC claims alone cannot observe never-filed losses. Stop here rather than infer these missing probabilities from weak aggregate correlations or expand collection without a concrete data lead.
