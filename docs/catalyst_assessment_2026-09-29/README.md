# Near-term catalyst assessment: aftermarket / Copart revenue

As of 29 September 2026. Scope: public information catalysts over the next one to two quarters; no earnings/DCF model, outreach or scheduled monitoring. Reused existing model audit, parts evidence and latest local transcript. Conclusion: several concrete observation windows exist, but no verified scheduled event guarantees aftermarket-specific repricing. Distinguish event certainty from outcome and stock-impact uncertainty.

## Starting point investors can already observe

Copart's September10 call reports US insurance unit sales down 7.5% but insurance ASP up3.7%; Q2 calendar2026 total-loss frequency23.3% versus22.4% prior year. Management says domestic assignments would have increased2.3% excluding one lost customer. Source: local `raw/transcripts/call_2026-09-10.txt`, lines90–115 and200–220; underlying licensed transcript preserved. This prevents attributing current weak units automatically to aftermarket repairs. Price resilience, rising totaling and carrier allocation are competing explanations. No independent survey of current investor expectations was performed.

## Ranked observation windows and required surprises

1. **Copart FY27 Q1 results, expected November2026; exact date unconfirmed.** Main test: weak organic US insurance assignments/sales persist after customer-loss, CAT and timing adjustments while insurance ASP growth sharply slows or turns negative, with weaker fee RPU or price-sensitive revenue. Stable comparable vehicle values would strengthen the donor-demand interpretation. Compare insurance and noninsurance, domestic and export commentary, fee changes and vehicle composition; do not treat an aggregate Manheim comparison as full adjustment. Management attribution to lower recycler bids or source substitution would substantially strengthen causal evidence. This would challenge a volume rebound AND the price cushion. It is a results catalyst because specific forward unit/RPU assumptions change, not because any EPS miss proves the thesis. Acquired and legacy revenue must remain separate.

2. **LKQ Q3 call, October29,2026,08:00 ET; confirmed.** Look for divergence between new aftermarket and recycled collision demand: recycled volume/pricing/sell-through weakens despite stable or improving repair activity, inventories age, and purchasing discipline reduces donor bids. Product-specific commentary is needed; consolidated revenue or EPS is inadequate. Existing Q2 release already reports alternative-parts utilization above40% and positive North American organic growth; simply repeating that is not new. Europe's ERP problems or an unrelated strategic-review headline do not validate this mechanism. A clear new disclosure could cause investors to anticipate Copart pricing weakness before Copart reports. No claim that such a disclosure is scheduled.

3. **Next public CCC/Mitchell claims update; release date/content unconfirmed.** Strong data surprise: total-loss frequency falls or undershoots a previously specified benchmark despite normalizing claims and stable vehicle values, alongside lower repair-cost growth and higher AM usage. Strengthen with age/loss-type controls and initial/final-estimate consistency; two compatible observations are more persuasive than one. A rising TLF can still be below counterfactual, but a model-inferred counterfactual is less visible and less likely to catalyze near-term market recognition. Higher AM share alone supports adoption, not donor impairment. CCC investor events page has no upcoming event as checked; do not invent a report date.

4. **A newly announced major-carrier/repair-network sourcing rule with an effective date; unscheduled candidate.** Best non-earnings structural catalyst: documented expansion of eligible AM categories, procurement requirement or rollout with identifiable claim exposure. Measure incremental scope versus today's policy, accepted/available supply and lead times. Current State Farm acceptance is existing policy, not a new catalyst. A new rule displacing only OEM supports repair savings but does not prove recycled-demand loss. No such new near-term announcement was verified in this pass.

## How the estimate revision becomes concrete

Hypothetical illustration, not current consensus: an analyst cuts next-year insurance sold units3% and insurance service RPU2% versus their previous forecast. That reduces that revenue block by4.94%: 0.97×0.98−1. The global-service effect equals the block effect times its correctly scoped revenue weight, with other branches separately modeled. No EPS or stock-price change follows mechanically. In the thesis, the critical revision is withdrawing assumed unit recovery plus reducing expected price-sensitive fee growth; a one-off tax, interest or CAT miss is not the same catalyst.

The current model's 3.7% price continuation is OUR assumption, not verified consensus. The old uniform $91m scenario is not a prediction of the next quarter after adopting a multi-year national sourcing path. Updated consensus/perimeter evidence is required before quoting a quantified expected miss.

## What should not be called a near-term catalyst

A three-year5.5 pp adoption scenario, a private CCC interview, a single Bidmate export, historical State Farm rollout, manufacturer shipment growth, or a general annual parts report without surprising relevant metrics. These can improve research confidence; they do not automatically produce public market recognition. ACV closing, international growth, service adoption and competitor share changes can move headline revenue independently and obscure the thesis. Aggregate EPS weakness may come from operating investments, acquisition effects, interest or other factors outside this revenue thesis.

## Tomorrow's CCC interview: find a public recognition path

Ask: (1) are national source shares, total-loss rates and near-threshold decisions showing a change in2026 rather than only2024–2025? (2) what public report will contain the relevant data and when is it expected? (3) are there already publicly announced carrier/network procurement changes with a future effective date? (4) can the apparent effect be separated from vehicle values, claim mix and repair completion? Do not treat private interview confidence as the public catalyst. No request to disclose confidential carrier plans is needed.
