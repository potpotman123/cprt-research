# Measuring the aftermarket transition: evidence and next decisions

29 September 2026. Revenue-model follow-up to the [audit](../model_audit_2026-09-29/AUDIT.md). **“The transition is unmeasured” was too broad.** Adoption, prices, availability and supplier activity have public observations. The unmeasured quantities are the matched recycled displacement, current claim-weighted exposure, near-threshold switching and transmission into auction prices. This pass adds usable historical price data and narrower present-day constraints; it does not validate the complete short thesis.

Existing CCC sourcing data, component-price pilot, calibration, recycler reporting schemas and auction feasibility failures were reused. No agents, outreach, paid data, bulk scraping, OCR, historical recalibration or extensive fitting. Read-only discovery spanned manufacturers, certifiers, insurer operating rules, parts marketplaces and component sellers. One small historical regression diagnostic and three existing-engine price sensitivities were executed because the new data directly challenge the original price assumption.

## What we can fit, and what the fit teaches us

We can also extract more information from the **existing** CCC count table without pretending its rounded values are exact. Under ordinary nearest-tenth rounding and a common appraisal population for category/total counts, AM's implied physical share rises from **23.08–23.99% in 2024** to **24.90–25.87% in 2025**, an increase bounded at approximately **0.92–2.79 percentage points**. These are rounding bounds, not statistical confidence intervals. The count table does not independently repeat every population exclusion; keep that compatibility assumption visible. This is a useful historical adoption constraint, but does not identify the counterfactual source displaced or become an incremental FY27 shock. See the preserved [CCC extraction](../repair_salvage_execution_2026-09-28/MIX_SEGMENTATION.md).

The newly saved `mitchell_discount_history.csv` contains all 35 rounded chart values with year, definition, page and URL. `calculate.py` fits ordinary two-parameter lines to each seven-point historical series. This costs little and is more informative than fitting a flexible adoption curve to two aggregate shares.

For domestic six-component discounts, with `t = year − 2010`, the descriptive fit is:

`discount_pct_of_OE = 31.382 − 1.518 × t`, in percentage points; in-sample `R² = 0.919`.

It describes those years, not the future. A retrospective diagnostic fits only 2010–2015 and predicts 2016. For domestic radiators it predicts **16.75%**, versus **25.8% observed**; simply retaining 2015's discount misses by only **2.4 pp**, versus **9.05 pp** for the trend. The flat benchmark beats the trend in **all five series** (the Asian comparison is very close relative to chart rounding). This was not a preregistered experiment and does not establish that all trends fail. It demonstrates why a high in-sample fit should not determine our current coefficient.

One or two observations are still useful when the remaining relationship is an identity:

`repair-bill saving = fraction of the original bill moved from OEM to AM × matched AM discount`.

For an **assumed 6%** of the original bill newly switched, the historical domestic bumper discount gives **1.23%** complete-bill savings; the radiator discount gives **1.55%**. These examples do not establish the 6% exposure or apply 2016 prices to a 2026 forecast. Price levels can be measured with a small matched basket; incidence/exposure must be separately estimated.

A fuller component accounting equation is:

`C = labor + paint + sublet/calibration + Σ components(q_O p_O + q_A p_A + q_R p_R) + incremental fitment/returns costs`.

This admits OEM competitive repricing as well as quantity substitution. The newer sources make that additional channel worth testing. An OEM-to-AM discount alone does **not** establish that AM is cheaper than recycled. Supply/catalogue growth can bound availability; it cannot separately identify adoption, installed demand and source displacement.

## Connection to the existing revenue model

The evidence belongs **upstream of the aftermarket adapter**, in component eligibility, source mix and relative delivered prices. The adapter then supplies repair costs and expected/realized salvage to the existing claim-selection and fee engines. We do not replace the revenue architecture or refit its historical calibration.

I applied two historical price ratios only as **diagnostic alternatives** to the original assumed AM/OEM ratio of 0.50. The eligible basket, future transfers (4 pp OEM and 1.5 pp recycled to AM), recycled price ratio 0.60, donor exposure and all bid-response assumptions stay fixed. The exercise deliberately reveals the consequences of changing one weak input; it is not an empirically assembled current basket.

| Price input in hypothetical basket | Complete repair cost | Insurance units vs reference | Insurance RPU vs reference | FY legacy-service delta |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Original assumed AM/OEM 0.50 | −1.030% | −2.057% | −0.890% | **−$90.66m** |
| Historical Asian 2016 ratio 0.701 | −0.472% | −1.516% | −1.036% | **−$78.50m** |
| Historical domestic 2016 ratio 0.767 | −0.302% | −1.351% | −1.080% | **−$74.81m** |

Smaller repair savings reduce the unit effect but also reduce the modeled positive rebuilder-bid offset; thus the RPU effect becomes more negative in these cases. Their remaining material downside still relies on **assumed recycled displacement and auction transmission**. This is robustness to one price assumption, not proof of the short. At the latter two price ratios, moving a part from the still-assumed 0.60-priced recycled source to AM actually raises that component's repair cost. That channel cannot be called unconditionally deflationary.

No central input or existing scenario output was overwritten. `historical_price_proxy_sensitivity.csv` and `results.json` preserve the new calculations. The original −$90.655866m case reproduces; revenue deltas reconcile. The only regression is the small descriptive diagnostic above.

## Practical resolution of audit questions 2–5

These numbers refer to the five-question summary in the conversation, rather than the priority codes in the detailed audit.

| Question | Cheapest useful step using existing/public material | Targeted observation that would resolve the main uncertainty | Admission / stopping rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| **2. Does calibration identify the total-loss response?** | Freeze the existing calibration. Compare its predicted changes with a simple aggregate benchmark and genuinely unused periods/metrics where definitions match. Produce a near-threshold-mass hurdle rather than another flexible fit. Inventory which years/targets were already used before designating a holdout. | For the CCC interview, request an existing aggregate histogram across **all claim dispositions** of `g = expected repair cost − (pre-loss value − expected net salvage)`, with total claim counts, comparable estimate maturity, age and loss type. Narrow bins around zero matter much more than a new national average. | If repair savings are `a` dollars and expected net salvage falls `b` dollars, the simple economic rule switches claims with **0 ≤ g < a+b**. Measure that mass, then compare with the model. Overrides and non-economic total losses remain separate. If no compatible histogram/threshold record exists, retain a bounded response range; a repaired-only sample or better training fit cannot close this gap. |
| **3. Which claims and donors are exposed?** | Build eligibility for **panels/bumpers, lighting/cooling, newer sensors, mechanical/powertrain and other**. Use CAPA availability, insurer acceptance and existing vehicle-age/body weights. Start with the six measured collision components; zero exposure elsewhere is a conservative scenario convention, not proof of immunity. | A modest matched component/interchange export: vehicle/model year, quantity, source, delivered allowed price, repair operation, initial/final estimate and fulfillment/return status. Weight by repair encounters and **donor contribution**, separately. | Catalogue/SKU counts measure coverage, not repair frequency. Apply eligibility once: do not multiply the same exclusion into both basket exposure and the current threshold-response control. Keep mechanical, scrap, export and rebuilder economics visible. Stop at the missing incident/contribution denominator rather than fill it with sales or crash-frequency proxies. |
| **4. Is the reference revenue forecast independently defensible?** | Build a clearly sourced baseline by US insurance, other US and international; separate reported unit/RPU changes from assumptions. Carry insurance allocation and international-growth alternatives alongside the complex engine. Use a simpler aggregate forecast as the benchmark. | Carrier assignment evidence or reconciled disclosed unit trends; fee-vintage/buyer-mix observations; a distinct international volume/price/FX bridge and an updated comparable service-revenue benchmark. | Do not explain residual RPU as service adoption. The current international continuation adds about **$89.91m**, almost the original aftermarket headwind. Baseline uncertainty must appear beside the thesis delta. Inferred insurance units remain calibrated if no independent count exists. No earnings/DCF work needed. |
| **5. When does revenue move, and what revenue is included?** | Add an explicit quarterly adoption path relative to already embedded sourcing, plus separate assignment-to-sale and insurer-expectation lags. Reuse existing conservation/recognition interfaces and delay cases. Keep legacy services, purchased revenue and acquired activity in separate ledgers. | Dated component introductions and insurer rollouts, plus actual assignment/sale vintage summaries or aggregate inventory durations; acquisition revenue classification/eliminations from the relevant disclosures. | A 2023 rollout is historical, not a new FY27 catalyst. Manufacturer shipments may lead installed use through inventory. Unknown acquisition classification stays null. Do not label an ex-acquisition service calculation total-company revenue or promise a near-term miss without a timing observation. |

Recommended order: **exposure and matched prices (3), threshold mass (2), independent reference (4), then quarterly timing/perimeter (5)**. The public-data portion and the accounting improvements can proceed without a large collection project. For empirical identification, one small existing CCC table and one recycler inventory/contribution table would be more valuable than a large archive of unmatched auction listings. Those are proposed requests; no interview results or recycler records have been received and no outreach was sent.

The best historical comparison candidate is an insurer/component acceptance change, examining affected and unaffected components in comparable claim cohorts before and after implementation. It requires pretrend checks, stable definitions, multiple pre/post observations and controls for vehicle values, claim severity and supply conditions. A policy announcement alone is an instrument for organizing research, not a proven exogenous causal shock.
