## New practitioner evidence for donor economics

Ron Sturgeon, “Inventory Turns and Purchasing,” Automotive Recycling, July–August 2018, p20, describes gross margins of 40–60% in his practitioner convention. He defines vehicle-purchase cost against own-stock parts sales, excluding brokered/new parts. This is dated practitioner guidance, not a representative statistical sample. [Original article](https://content.yudu.com/web/2y3n7/Auto-Recyclers-BG/JulAug18/html/20.html).

Conditional accounting check: if acquisition cost/sales c is 0.40–0.60, and v is variable fulfillment cost/sales, net contribution/acquisition cost is (1−v)/c. A 1.5× ratio requires v=1−1.5c, or 10–40%. Those costs are REQUIRED values, not measured here. Acquisition cost also need not equal hammer price. Thus the evidence makes the economic scale interpretable; it does not establish 1.5×.

Car-Part's [Bidmate](https://products.car-part.com/bidmate/) explicitly uses projected sales, requests and inventory to recommend purchases and supports overhead calculations. This validates the choice of operational records to request, not a universal bid coefficient.

Fenix's [2016 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1615153/000161515317000077/fenx-20161231x10k.htm), Financial Statement Components, reports direct purchasing/dismantling labor and overhead at approximately 23% of COGS; distribution and selling costs also occur below COGS. Its 86% recycled/related sales versus 14% ancillary revenue is not the collision-versus-mechanical split. Neither metric identifies 25% exposed net contribution or the exact hammer-price ratio. Mixing them with another operator's margin would create a synthetic estimate rather than identify ours.

The 25% exposure remains unsupported numerically. Broader collision market size, body-shop customer share, component counts and gross sales mix are not interchangeable with exposed net contribution. Do not manufacture a matching 25% from those denominators. Required record: same-donor expected sold component proceeds net of costs, with aftermarket-eligible collision components separately identified, plus unaffected recoveries.

## Discount provenance

Original prices OEM=1, AM=0.50, recycled=0.60 were hypothetical. They imply a 50% AM/OEM discount and a 16.67% AM/recycled discount. The latter has no matched national supporting dataset.

Already verified [Mitchell Q2 2017 report](https://www.mitchell.com/industry-trends-report/itr-2017-q2-apd/offline/download.pdf), pp8–10, gives 2016 six-component AM discounts of 23.3% domestic, 29.9% Asian, 27.7% European. These are historical origin/component aggregates, not current fixed-interchange transactions. Local extracted observations and source cache remain available; live web PDF retrieval failed this follow-up, so no new extraction is claimed.

[MapleV's September 2026 catalogue comparison](https://maplev.ca/guides/tesla-collision-part-prices-canada/) reports 259 matched Tesla part numbers with aggregate AM list prices around half OEM. This is a vendor-selected Canadian Tesla catalogue, not claim-weighted US allowed prices. Some components are not cheaper. It demonstrates that 50% is possible in a particular population, not fair as a universal parameter.

No evidence supports applying either a 50% or a 25% component discount to an entire repair bill. Complete-bill savings equal original bill dollars actually switched times the matched discount, minus incremental fitment/returns/labor costs. Repairing an existing component is a separate alternative and needs labor/material/time data; no measured repair-versus-replacement cost coefficient exists in this adapter.

## What the current forecast actually does

It holds source price ratios constant, imposes full-year incremental transfers uniformly across the FY27 quarters, and solves selection/auction feedback. It does not forecast a discount trend, estimate an adoption curve or compound a historical 1% repair-cost decline. Reference price growth and other engine assumptions are separate.

The larger case shifts 4 pp OEM and 1.5 pp recycled to AM: hypothetical AM share 25%→30.5%, a 5.5 pp rise. Its 1.03% whole-bill saving is the output of that choice, the assumed discounts and 40% exposure.

Existing CCC evidence: AM spending share rose 21.0%→22.7% (+1.7 pp), while rounded count ratios imply roughly 23.5%→25.4% (+1.9 pp). Preserved rounding bounds put the latter increase around 0.92–2.79 pp under common-population assumptions. These quantities describe different denominators from the hypothetical eligible basket. They do not justify a 5.5 pp future increase or identify its recycled origin. The observed 2024–25 change is already historical and must not be reapplied as incremental FY27 savings.

Within the OLD hypothetical basket, moving just 1 pp OEM→AM saves 0.2395% of the complete bill: 0.40×0.01×(1−0.50)/0.835. This is a magnitude diagnostic only. Moving 1 pp recycled→AM instead saves 0.0479%. The source displaced matters as much as total AM growth.

## Defensible projection method and admission rule

1. Start with latest measured source quantities and prices for matched component/vehicle cohorts. Separate all-parts share, eligible spending and actual adoption.
2. A no-additional-adoption case is a transparent neutral benchmark, not an empirical forecast of zero adoption. Add explicitly conditional continuation and acceleration cases only on compatible denominators; do not substitute national dollar-share changes for eligible-basket physical shifts.
3. Project adoption separately from price discounts. A constant matched-price ratio is a stated simplifying assumption; a changing ratio needs repeated matched observations, tariff/FX/input-cost evidence or competitive repricing data. The earlier historical trend diagnostic failed to outperform a flat benchmark and should not be extrapolated.
4. Use an explicit quarterly rollout and completion path; without timing observations, retain annual stress cases rather than promise a specific quarterly miss.
5. Keep recycled displacement and donor economics unadmitted until supported. A complex engine cannot replace missing exposures, costs or marginal bidder evidence.
