# CCC director interview: the numbers that would change the model

Prepared 29 September 2026 for the user's interview tomorrow. Proposed requests only; no outreach sent and no answers assumed. Ask for existing aggregate exports first, not a bespoke giant data collection.

Opening framing: We want to distinguish cheaper sourcing on the same repair from changes in vehicles, severity and repair-versus-total-loss selection. We have your age/body frequencies and total-loss mix; the biggest missing inputs now concern components, realized savings and borderline dispositions.

## 1. Clarify the supplied workbook (two minutes)

- Are frequencies and total-loss mix from the identical US claim population? Which coverages, exclusions, carrier sample, estimate dates and maturity? Are 2025 estimates fully developed?
- Does Van include commercial vans? How are Utility Vehicle and current/newer defined?
- Does a repairable estimate mean a completed repair? Can one claim have multiple estimates, and are source quantities latest-snapshot or accumulated lines?

## 2. Highest-priority table: component source and price

Could you provide an existing 2024–2025 quarterly table, by component family and vehicle age, showing unique claim count, replacement quantity, allowed part dollars, and total repair dollars, with OEM, optional OEM, aftermarket, recycled and reconditioned separate? Start with bumper covers, hoods, fenders, lamps and cooling components; other categories can be grouped.

Within the same vehicle/component or interchange, can we see source availability, quoted prices, chosen source and fulfilled source? Please separate initial estimates from final supplements, cancellations/returns, freight and additional fitment labor. An aggregate matched-pair price table is valuable if line-level records cannot be shared.

Why: replaces the assumed source mix and measures actual whole-bill savings. Catalogue availability is different from insurer permission, quoting and installed use.

Useful checks to put on the table, without treating them as answers:

- Figure 39's 2025 rounded counts imply about 25.4% AM and 3.8% recycled by count across all replacement parts; recycled spending is 10.5%. Is a recycled assembly counted differently from individual new components?
- Figure 35's rounded all-parts/bill shares are 44%, 43%, 41%, 36% across the four age groups. What fraction of those dollars actually has an approved, available AM alternative?
- When AM availability grows, does it displace OEM, recycled, repaired operations or price-matched OEM? Can observed source changes distinguish those paths?

## 3. Highest-priority disposition table: near the threshold

Do you observe expected completed repair cost, pre-loss actual cash value, expected NET salvage and final disposition for the same claim? If so, request an existing histogram across ALL dispositions of:

`gap = expected repair cost − (pre-loss value − expected net salvage)`.

Ask for narrow bins around zero, e.g. $0–250, $250–500, $500–1,000 and corresponding negative bins, by vehicle age and loss type, with claim counts. These dollar bins are proposed reporting cuts, not calibrated thresholds. Ask whether rental, supplements, statutory tests and non-economic overrides change the actual decision rule. If fields are not observed, ask which are imputed and who would have the operational record.

Why: the model switches some borderline totals when repair cost or expected salvage falls; average total-loss frequency alone cannot establish how many. Ask how much source repricing historically moved otherwise comparable claims across disposition.

## 4. The offset: what do newly repaired vehicles buy?

For claims initially considered totals but ultimately repaired, are final repair completion, parts quantities and source mix observable? How do they compare with ordinary repairs? What fraction never completes repair, is cash-settled or is delayed? Are source savings ultimately lost through supplements or returns?

Why: our current offset assumes 100% repair completion and average recycled parts intensity. Near-threshold repairs may create materially more demand per claim.

## 5. Timing and a useful historical comparison

Is there a documented carrier/component AM acceptance rollout or catalogue expansion for which CCC has comparable before/after claims and an unaffected comparison group? Could an existing analysis report source mix, final repair costs and total-loss rates with severity and vehicle mix held reasonably constant? Request multiple pre/post periods and acknowledge simultaneous changes. A rollout date alone does not establish causality.

## Questions better directed to a recycler or salvage buyer

Do not ask CCC to guess the model's 25% and 1.5×. Ask whether it can refer us to the right operational data owner (no referral outreach is authorized here).

Needed donor-cohort table: hammer price, buyer fees, transport, component inventory, realized sale dates/prices, unsold items, returns, dismantling/fulfillment costs and scrap recoveries. This identifies exposed net contribution, timing and the denominator for bid capacity. Bid histories or buyer interviews are separately needed for marginal recycler versus rebuilder competition.

If interview time is short: prioritize the matched component/source table and the all-disposition threshold distribution. The former measures the repair shock; the latter measures how strongly it changes units. Neither alone proves an auction-price decline.

## Follow-up: testing the chosen 5.5-point scenario

Ask whether any matched component/carrier/age subgroup shows an observed 5.5 percentage-point increase in aftermarket quantity share over a year. What fraction of all replacement quantities and bill dollars does that subgroup represent? Which source lost share? Include unchanged groups. This tests concentration rather than equating national spending growth with physical adoption.
