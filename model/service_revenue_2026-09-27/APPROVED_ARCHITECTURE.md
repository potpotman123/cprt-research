# User-approved model architecture

Confirmed by the user on 27 September 2026 after reviewing the quarterly workbook. Preserve this organization while Fable handles further visual design. The approval covers organization, not empirical validation or final styling.

## Four main reading tabs

1. **RPM Summary:** reported and projected quarterly US, international and total service revenue in dollars, including the near-term half-year view.
2. **Volume Build:** fleet cohorts, claim exposure, total-loss frequency and unit/RPU growth contributions.
3. **Vehicle Economics:** body-specific repair costs, pre-loss value, recovery, total-loss thresholds and fee economics.
4. **Revenue Bridge:** explicit conversion of the demographic engine into quarterly service revenue against a stated baseline.

Keep the live detailed engines behind those views: Cohort Roll, Repair Costs, Loss Calibration and Fee Calculation. Keep source records separate in AD Financials, AD Fleet, AD Repair and AD Fees & TLF. CALCULATIONS and DATA mark the supporting sections. Four main views does not mean four total tabs.

Only section markers have colored tabs. Main schedules are compact tables with aligned periods, clear dividing lines and limited whitespace. Do not remove equations or turn calculations into fixed outputs to simplify presentation. The reference workbooks still govern subsequent visual design; the user has not approved the current imitation of their styling.

Current scope ends at total service revenue. Do not add EPS, a DCF, vehicle-sales revenue or CapEx without a new request. Preserve sources, population definitions, proxy labels, assumptions and methodological limitations. Fable may change formatting while analytical work continues here.

## Next analytical priorities proposed, not yet executed

- Attribute the forecast to age, body mix, changing within-cohort economics and their interactions using explicit counterfactuals. Distinguish a structural level difference from a six-month growth difference.
- Test the assumption that one unconditional body-average auction value represents the subset of vehicles actually totaled. Repair severity, totaling probability and conditional salvage proceeds may move together.
- Revisit within-age TLF changes before attributing recent TLF growth mainly to aging. Existing CCC decomposition suggests a larger within-age component, with population-compatibility caveats.
- Validate on an earlier-to-later period with inputs and calibration fixed at the earlier information date; fitting the same outcome being explained is not validation.
- Compare matched-scope quarterly service forecasts with a dated external forecast before claiming an earnings-revision opportunity. Latest reported growth is currently only a mechanical baseline.

Begin with bounded local arithmetic and existing records. Ask before large collection, paid data, broad OCR or computationally expensive exploration. Stronger granularity should make an economically material mechanism testable, not merely increase the number of assumptions.
