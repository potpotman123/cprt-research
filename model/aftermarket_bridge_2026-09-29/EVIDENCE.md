## Two arithmetic checks that prevent overstating the data

1. At constant total replacement spending, recycled's dollar-share change implies `10.5/10.7−1 = −1.87%` recycled dollars. **Total replacement spending growth of 1.90% exactly cancels that share loss.** To infer market demand also requires repair-job volume and compatible populations. No actual recycled-dollar series was manufactured by combining mismatched published averages.
2. Assuming ordinary nearest-tenth rounding, a displayed recycled count of 0.6 followed by 0.5 permits an underlying decrease approaching **0% to 30.8%**, before sample and selection effects. The familiar 16.7% calculation is not a precisely measured rate and cannot calibrate the model's 15% displacement scenario.

The first is a spending identity; the second is a rounding bound. Neither is a causal substitution estimate. The model's initial 10% recycled **physical** share is hypothetical and must not be confused with CCC's 10.7% recycled **dollar** share.

## Minimum evidence needed to upgrade the pitch

**CCC director interview — request, not data received:** an existing aggregate export for two comparable periods, by component/interchange, vehicle age and pre-disposition damage cohort, showing OEM/aftermarket/recycled quantities, allowed delivered prices and fulfillment/returns. Include repaired and totaled claims, comparable estimate maturity, expected net salvage and initial/supplemented repair-to-value gaps. Ask explicitly how much aftermarket growth replaces OEM versus recycled. A matched repaired-only panel improves pricing but still cannot identify the total-loss response.

**Recycler route:** existing component sell-through, net price/contribution after variable costs, inventory age, return/fill rates and donor maximum-bid records for the same vehicle/component cohorts. Separate mechanical demand, collision demand and scrap. Link changes to source competition, controlling repair-job volume, stock and donor mix. No contact or access assumed.

**Auction transmission:** comparable bid distributions or evidence about marginal bidders, alongside donor supply. Winning-buyer shares alone do not identify how a recycler's lower maximum bid changes hammer prices. Expected insurer recovery revisions need a separate timing observation.

A small existing export or aggregate table is preferable to a bulk archive scrape: it measures the decision and denominator directly, with less collection and matching cost. Until available, retain scenario ranges and the materiality hurdle. No quantitative empirical coefficient was admitted in this pass; direct substitution, marginal switching mass and price transmission remain missing.
